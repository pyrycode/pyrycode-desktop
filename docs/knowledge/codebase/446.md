proves queue-while-busy-then-drop against a **genuinely running** turn — a real daemon holding a

## Patterns established

- **Drop-before-drain is proved by bracketing the drop with two identical running-affordance checks,
  not by timing.** `interruptButton` visible immediately before the drop click and again immediately
  after the row leaves the backlog — both while turn 1 is still mid-turn — establishes that the
  dequeue happened *within* turn 1's lifetime, so the row could not have left via a drain (drain only
  fires at turn end). This is the deterministic, DOM-only answer to the #442-class dequeue-before-drain
  hazard: no wire-frame ordering is available on this tier (the daemon is a separate process), so the
  proof has to come from two black-box state reads that bracket the mutation instead.
- **A long, cheap prompt is the load-bearing timing knob for the whole spec**, not incidental
  padding — it has to outlast both the enqueue/drop round-trip (a handful of relay hops, ~1–2s) *and*
  the drain-to-quiesce wait, while still finishing inside `TURN_TIMEOUT_MS` (120s). Reused the #445
  twin's exact prompt shape ("Count from 1 to 300, one number per line") for consistency across the
  two real-claude turn-coupled siblings, rather than inventing a new one.
- **The optimistic `user`-role echo and the `queued`-role backlog row are two independent DOM
  reflections of the same send and must be asserted separately.** `submitMessage` fires an
  unconditional optimistic echo regardless of turn phase, so a mid-turn send always renders both a
  timeline `data-thread-role="user"` row and (once `queue_state` arrives) a `data-thread-role="queued"`
  backlog row. Scoping every queue assertion to `.conversation__queued` / `data-thread-role="queued"`
  keeps the two from colliding; the echo is expected to **remain** after the drop (only the queue
  entry is removed) and must not be asserted away.
- **Proving "no second turn ran" reuses the #445 twin's two-signal quiesce + fixed-settle-window
  idiom verbatim** (`interruptButton` count-0 **and** `.bubble__cursor` count-0, baseline captured
  strictly after both fire, then a 3s `SETTLE_MS` re-read) — the same negative-proof shape generalizes
  cleanly from "no more chunks after interrupt" to "no chunks at all for a turn that was dropped before
  it ever started."
